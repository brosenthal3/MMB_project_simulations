# 2D phase-field model of epithelial cancer cell invasion
This repository contains code used to simulate cancer cell invasion using a phase-field model. 
This project was part of the Multiscale Mathematical Biology course at Leiden University (taken 2025-2026). 

The work was performed by Ben Rosenthal & Mai Nguyen, both following a minor in Quantitative Biology.

## Project summary
The study was written in a report with the title: **Effects of differential division rates and adhesion strengths in a 2D phase-field model of epithelial cancer cell invasion**.

### Abstract
Epithelial homeostasis depends on the regulation of cell division, cell-cell adhesion, and apical extrusion, 
while disruptions to these processes are closely linked to cancer invasion and growth. 
Phase-field models have been widely used to study multicellular mechanics and cell extrusion, 
yet the role of increased division rates in combination with differential adhesion has not been widely explored. 
In this study, we extend an existing two-dimensional phase-field model of epithelial tissue by incorporating stochastic, type-dependent cell division. 
The model describes healthy and cancer cells stochastically dividing and mechanically interacting with each other and with an extracellular matrix (ECM) through surface tension, volume constraints, and adhesion. By systematically varying the cancer cell division rate and cancer cell-ECM adhesion strength, we investigate their effects on cancer volume, basal invasion depth, and cell death. In the simulations, increased cancer cell–ECM adhesion significantly reduced apical extrusion and cell death, but did not enhance basal invasion depth. In contrast, higher cancer cell division rates led to increased ECM invasion depth as well as increased cell death, indicating that proliferation promotes both basal and apical extrusion. Within this model, increased proliferation rates play a critical role in cancer cell invasion from the epithelial layer to the basal membrane, 
while increased ECM adhesion alone is not enough to facilitate deeper basal extrusion.
